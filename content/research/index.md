My research lies at the intersection of numerical analysis, partial differential equations, and scientific computing. I am interested in numerical methods whose stability and accuracy can be understood through rigorous analysis.

## Boussinesq systems

My doctoral research investigated finite element Galerkin approximations to the Boussinesq system of equations. This work includes a three-step two-level method and discontinuous Galerkin approximations for transient heat-conducting fluid flow.

The central questions are how to construct efficient discretizations, derive a priori bounds, and obtain error estimates for the velocity, pressure, and temperature.

## Discontinuous Galerkin methods

I study discontinuous Galerkin and local discontinuous Galerkin formulations for nonlinear evolution problems. My interests include spatial discretization, time discretization, two-grid methods, and the relationship between numerical stability and analytical estimates.

## Viscoelastic fluid flow

My work on the Kelvin–Voigt model uses local discontinuous Galerkin approximations to study viscoelastic fluid flow, including semidiscrete and fully discrete error analysis.

## Current directions

I am exploring robust fully discrete DG methods for Boussinesq systems and the long-time behavior of DG approximations to fourth-order semilinear evolution problems. These directions involve adaptive time stepping, discrete energy estimates, and uniform decay with respect to the mesh parameter.
